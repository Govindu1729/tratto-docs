Tratto is built on EU infrastructure by default, not retrofitted for compliance. This page covers what that means in practice, and what to expect if you're evaluating Tratto for a GDPR-sensitive workload.

## EU-Native Infrastructure

Tratto runs on Google Cloud's EU region. Your data (contacts, email content, event logs) is stored and processed there, not exported to the US and synced back.

## Cookie Consent & Analytics

The Tratto marketing site loads no analytics scripts by default. Google Analytics 4 (via Google Tag Manager) only activates after a visitor explicitly accepts the cookie banner, and is configured to avoid collecting personally identifiable information.

## Unsubscribe and the Preference Page
Every campaign and flow email offers the recipient two ways out, and both work
without any infrastructure on your side.

**One-click unsubscribe in the mail client.** Campaign and flow emails carry
the `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers, so mail clients
that support one-click unsubscribe (Gmail, Outlook and others) show their own
unsubscribe button. The header always points at Tratto's API, never at a web
page, and a click unsubscribes the contact at once. Transactional emails sent
with `POST /v1/emails` are not tied to a contact and do not carry these headers.

**The preference page.** The `{{unsubscribe_url}}` link in the email footer
opens a page on `app.tratto.email/preferences` where the recipient can:

- **unsubscribe** from every email of that sender;
- **turn off open and click tracking** and keep receiving emails (see
  [below](#tracking-pixel-consent-garante-privacy-order-no-2842026));
- **subscribe again**, if they had unsubscribed. This is not offered to an
  address suppressed after a bounce or a spam complaint: those stay suppressed,
  and the page tells the recipient to contact the sender.

Each link is signed and belongs to one contact, so the page needs no login.
Unsubscribe links in emails sent before the page moved to `app.tratto.email`
still work: they redirect to it. If your workspace has set its own unsubscribe
page (`customUnsubscribeUrl` on `PATCH /v1/workspace`), the footer link goes
there instead, with `contact` and `email` added to the query string; the
one-click header keeps pointing at Tratto.

Unsubscribing, from either path, moves the contact to `unsubscribed` and
cancels the emails still waiting to go to them (they count as `skipped` in the
campaign stats). See [Contacts](/en/docs/contacts) for the API.

<Callout>
Unsubscribing stops future sends immediately. It does not delete the contact
record: see below.
</Callout>

## Tracking Pixel Consent (Garante Privacy Order No. 284/2026)

Italy's Data Protection Authority (Garante Privacy), Order No. 284 of 17 April 2026 (doc. web no. 10241943), sets out obligations for anyone using tracking pixels in email. If you send campaigns or flows through Tratto, three obligations apply to you as the sender:

1. **Clear, transparent notice**: recipients must be told that opens and clicks are tracked.
2. **Free, specific consent**: general newsletter sign-up consent isn't enough, tracking needs its own consent.
3. **Granular revocation**: recipients must be able to withdraw tracking consent easily, without being forced to unsubscribe from everything.

**What Tratto does technically**: the open pixel (a transparent 1×1 GIF) and click tracking only fire for emails tied to a known contact (campaigns, flows), never for pure transactional sends like a password reset, where there's no engagement purpose to justify it. The open event never carries the recipient's address, only an internal identifier resolved server-side. Opens and clicks are recorded only within 30 days of sending: after that, a tracked link still takes the recipient to its destination, but the click is not recorded.

<Callout type="warning">
The notice and specific consent toward your own recipients are your responsibility, not Tratto's: you're the controller for your campaigns (see below). What Tratto provides is the granular revocation in point 3.
</Callout>

**Revoking tracking consent**: the [preference page](#unsubscribe-and-the-preference-page) has a separate switch that turns tracking off while the recipient stays subscribed. From then on, campaign and flow emails reach that recipient with no open pixel and no click tracking: both stop together. The choice applies to emails sent after it, and the recipient can turn tracking back on from the same page.

What this means for you as the sender:

- The contact shows `trackingOptOut: true` in the [Contacts API](/en/docs/contacts) and in the dashboard.
- You can set the opt-out yourself (for example, to honour a request that reached you by email), but you cannot clear it: `PATCH /v1/contacts/:id` with `trackingOptOut: false` on a contact who opted out answers `403 FORBIDDEN`. Only the recipient can turn tracking back on.
- Campaign stats count these recipients as `untracked` and leave them out of the open and click rates, which are never estimated for them: see [Campaigns](/en/docs/campaigns).

## Controller vs. Processor

Who's responsible for a data subject request depends on who sent the email:

- **Tratto sent it directly**: waitlist confirmations, account/product emails, our own marketing. Tratto is the data controller. See [Data Subject Rights](#data-subject-rights-tratto-as-controller) below.
- **A Tratto tenant sent it**: a business using Tratto's API to send their own transactional or marketing email. Tratto is the data **processor** acting on that tenant's behalf; the tenant is the data controller for their own recipients. See [If a Tenant Sent You an Email](#if-a-tenant-sent-you-an-email) below.

If you're not sure which applies, check who the email is *from*: that's the controller.

## Data Subject Rights (Tratto as Controller)

For emails Tratto sends directly (waitlist, account, our own marketing), you can request access to, rectification of, or deletion of your data by writing to **privacy@tratto.email**.

## If a Tenant Sent You an Email

If you received a transactional or marketing email sent *by one of Tratto's customers* through our API, Tratto processed that email on their behalf but does not control it: the sending business is the data controller and is responsible for honoring your access, rectification, or erasure request within the timeframe required by applicable law (GDPR: generally one month). Contact the sender directly, not Tratto.

<Callout type="warning">
**Note for tenants**: the [Contacts API](/en/docs/contacts) currently supports suppressing a contact (`status: unsubscribed`), not deleting the record: there's no self-service erasure endpoint yet. If one of your recipients submits a GDPR erasure request, Tratto doesn't yet give you a self-service way to fully purge that contact's data. Talk to us at **privacy@tratto.email** if this is a blocker for your compliance workflow.
</Callout>
